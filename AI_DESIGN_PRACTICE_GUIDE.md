## The Golden Rule for Interviews (Honesty + Seniority)

### The Honest Truth:
Your past projects (like *MakeMyTrip* in 2020, or *GLA / Kingston MA* in 2023) were executed using classical, rigorous design methods: participatory workshops, sticky-note affinity mapping, Adobe Creative Suite, and manual wireframing. Modern Generative AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Figma AI) either did not exist or were not mature then.

### The 30-Second Elevator Pitch to Memorize:
> *"I treat AI as a high-speed thinking partner and workflow accelerator—not a replacement for taste, craft, or human empathy.  
> While my foundational projects were built through classical studio craft and in-person community workshops, over the past year I have integrated modern AI tools (Claude, Figma AI, Midjourney) into my active workflow. This section demonstrates how I apply modern AI workflows to solve real design challenges: from accessible token systems to qualitative research clustering and visual concept discovery."*

If an interviewer asks: **"Did you use AI on that 2020/2023 project back then?"**  
**Your Answer:**  
> *"No, back in 2020/2023 those tools didn't exist yet! Those projects were 100% manual field research, participatory facilitation, and vector craft. What I'm showcasing here is my **current design methodology**—taking real-world systemic design problems from my domain background and demonstrating how I tackle them 3x faster and more rigorously with modern AI today."*

## The 4 AI Use Cases Explained in Simple English

---

### Use Case 01: Accessibility & Design Systems
* **Labels:** `01 // ACCESSIBILITY` · `Figma AI · WCAG Tokens`
* **Headline:** **Semantic Palette & Contrast Matrix**

#### In Simple English:
Generating beautiful brand color schemes and immediately testing them so people with low vision or color-blindness can easily read everything on both light and dark screens.

#### How a Designer Actually Uses It:
1. **The Problem:** In manual design systems, calculating contrast ratios for 20+ color combinations across buttons, cards, and backgrounds takes hours of clicking through WebAIM contrast calculators.
2. **The AI Workflow:** You prompt Figma AI or token plugins with a mood (*e.g., "warm civic community"* or *"precision industrial tech"*). The AI proposes semantic color tokens (Primary, Secondary, Surface, Text).
3. **The Automated Check:** Built-in checks verify that text against background meets **WCAG AAA standards (minimum 7:1 ratio)** before any UI is coded.

#### What to Say in an Interview:
> *"I use AI to build inclusive design foundations from day one. Instead of manually testing contrast ratios after components are already designed, I use AI-assisted token generators in Figma to produce semantic palettes that are mathematically guaranteed to pass WCAG AAA contrast requirements across all client themes."*

---

### Use Case 02: Qualitative Research & Workshop Synthesis
* **Labels:** `02 // RESEARCH` · `Claude · Qualitative Synthesis`
* **Headline:** **Transcripts to Strategic Insights**

#### In Simple English:
Taking hundreds of messy quotes, sticky notes, and interview transcripts from community workshops, and using Claude to quickly group them into clear themes and actionable design briefs.

#### How a Designer Actually Uses It:
1. **The Problem:** After running a 2-hour participatory design workshop with 30 residents (like in the *Food of Us* project), you have 150+ sticky notes and voice recordings. Manually grouping them takes 3 full days.
2. **The AI Workflow:** You feed the anonymized raw quotes into Claude with a prompt: *"Group these citizen comments into recurring emotional barriers, and draft 'How Might We' opportunity statements for each group."*
3. **The Designer's Human Role:** AI does the heavy clustering in 60 seconds; you as the designer review, eliminate hallucinations, verify cultural nuances, and pick the most promising strategic directions.

#### What to Say in an Interview:
> *"Qualitative research produces huge volumes of unstructured text. I use Claude as a synthesis partner to process workshop transcripts and citizen feedback. It clusters raw quotes into affinity themes and drafts initial 'How Might We' statements, cutting synthesis time by 60% while leaving the strategic decision-making and human empathy entirely in my hands."*

### Use Case 03: UI/UX Flow Architecture & Edge-Case Discovery
*(Replaces the confusing 'Circular Service Loop' with an intuitive UI/UX concept)*
* **Labels:** `03 // UI/UX SYSTEMS` · `Claude · Edge-Case Architecture`
* **Headline:** **State Matrix & User Journey Edge Cases**

#### Why We Replaced Circular Loops:
"Circular service loops" sounded like a backend algorithmic supply-chain simulation. Since you are a **Visual & UI/UX Designer**, recruiters want to see how you design **user journeys, interface states, and product flows**.

#### In Simple English:
Thinking through all the "unhappy paths" and forgotten screens (empty cart, network failure, error modals, loading states) before building the final Figma wireframes.

#### How a Designer Actually Uses It:
1. **The Problem:** Junior designers often only design the "happy path" (e.g., the user clicks book, pays, and succeeds). But when developers build it, they ask: *"What if the internet drops? What if the inventory runs out during payment? What if this text is translated into German and overflows the button?"*
2. **The AI Workflow:** When designing a booking flow (like *MakeMyTrip* trek booking) or catalog (like *Synnova*), you prompt Claude: *"Act as a QA lead. Here is my 4-step user journey. Identify 8 critical edge cases, failure states, and empty states that need dedicated UI components."*
3. **The Designer's Human Role:** You design the specific visual component states (Default, Active, Loading, Disabled, Error, Empty) in Figma so developers have zero ambiguity.

#### What to Say in an Interview:
> *"Senior UI/UX design is defined by how well you handle failure states, not just the happy path. I use AI to stress-test my user journeys before wireframing. It acts as a QA thinking partner, predicting friction points, offline scenarios, and edge-case states so I can deliver complete, bulletproof component matrices to the engineering team."*

---

### Use Case 04: Visual Concept Discovery & Latent Metaphors
* **Labels:** `04 // DIRECTION` · `GenAI · Visual Discovery`
* **Headline:** **Latent Metaphor Exploration**

#### In Simple English:
Using Midjourney or Adobe Firefly to explore unexpected visual ideas and metaphors during the first 48 hours of brand exploration, then drawing the final geometric logo manually in Illustrator.

#### How a Designer Actually Uses It:
1. **The Problem:** When designing a logo (e.g. for a food co-op or an industrial gear brand), designers often get stuck on cliché ideas (a literal spoon, a literal gear, a green leaf).
2. **The AI Workflow:** You prompt text-to-image models with conceptual prompts: *"Communal stew pot, circular loop, hand as a bowl, woodcut print textures"* or *"Planetary gear teeth, mathematical kinetic orbit, minimalism"*.
3. **The Designer's Human Role:** AI does NOT create the logo. AI creates unexpected moodboards. You study the imagery, spot a compelling abstract relationship (e.g., *the bowl as an open hand*), and then jump into Adobe Illustrator to draw a mathematically clean, vector production mark from scratch.

#### What to Say in an Interview:
> *"I never use AI to generate final logos or finished artwork. Instead, I use it in the first 48 hours of brand discovery to break conventional visual tropes. Prompting latent metaphors helps me explore unexpected conceptual moodboards quickly. Once the client aligns on the creative direction, I take over completely in Illustrator to craft the geometric vector mark from scratch."*

## 3 Core Boundaries to Mention in Any Interview

1. **"AI generates ideas, humans have taste."**  
   AI gives you 50 options; your value as a designer with academic training (BVA Baroda, MA Kingston) is curation, cultural nuance, and visual hierarchy.
2. **"Zero proprietary data exposure."**  
   You sanitize and anonymize all research data before running prompts.
3. **"Vector craft is 100% human."**  
   Final client deliverables (logos, design tokens, responsive layouts) are hand-crafted, clean, and scalable.
